This document is the source of truth for the Orivion website redesign.

1. Company Formation
2. Trade Licensing & Amendments
3. PRO & Government Services
4. Compliance & Regulatory
5. Accounting & Tax
6. Banking Support

8. Corporate Changes & Additional Services

3. Product Design & Creative

## Editorial and expertise standard

Every service and sub-service must be written as if prepared by an experienced consultant who understands the business problem, the operating context and the technical or regulatory implications.

The copy must:
- define the service clearly before trying to sell it;
- explain when and why a business would need it;
- distinguish deliverables, responsibilities and dependencies;
- explain the process in commercially meaningful terms;
- identify important decisions, risks, prerequisites and trade-offs;
- state what Orivion does directly and where specialist, regulatory, legal, audit, tax or third-party involvement may be required;
- avoid vague agency language such as “cutting-edge”, “revolutionary”, “seamless”, “transform your business”, or unsupported claims of superiority;
- avoid invented statistics, customer counts, client logos, testimonials, rankings, guarantees, success rates or case studies;
- never guarantee approvals controlled by banks, government authorities, platforms, advertising networks or other third parties;
- use current authoritative sources for laws, thresholds, platform capabilities, compliance requirements and other facts that may change;
- explain technical terms in intelligent plain English without sounding simplistic;
- use natural professional English and avoid obvious AI-writing habits, formulaic triads, excessive em dashes, inflated language and repetitive generic headings;
- write with enough depth that a serious business owner, founder, manager or technical buyer can understand what they are purchasing.

## Website content standard for each service page

Each service page should normally cover:
1. What the service is
2. The business problem it addresses
3. When the service is appropriate
4. What Orivion can provide
5. Detailed sub-services and deliverables
6. How the engagement works
7. Inputs, prerequisites and client responsibilities
8. Important considerations and limitations
9. Technologies, platforms or authorities involved, where relevant
10. Expected outputs and handover
11. Related Orivion services
12. Frequently asked questions
13. Consultation or next-step CTA

Corporate-service pages should additionally explain relevant requirements, documents, authorities, dependencies and regulatory boundaries.

Technology-service pages should additionally explain use cases, architecture or platform choices, integrations, data/security considerations, delivery method, testing, deployment and ongoing support where relevant.
